01 / ORIENTATION

Why does media matter?

Media is more than a way to pass time. It is a system through which people learn about events, exchange ideas, express culture, and make decisions.

What is media and information literacy?

Media and Information Literacy (MIL) is the ability to find, access, understand, evaluate, use, and create information responsibly. It encourages people to ask who produced a message, what evidence supports it, why it was shared, and how it may affect an audience.

A media-literate person does not automatically believe or reject a message. Instead, they examine its source, context, purpose, and evidence before forming a judgment or sharing it.

The museum's central idea

Every medium has a message—and a limitation.

No single form of media can serve every purpose perfectly. A printed guide may be useful for careful reading, while a live broadcast may be better for urgent updates. Choosing wisely means understanding what each medium can and cannot do.

02 / THE EXHIBITS

Six galleries of media

Explore the forms of media we encounter in school, at home, in public spaces, and online. Each exhibit includes examples, strengths, and weaknesses.

Gallery 01 · Printed communication▤

Print Media

Print media communicates through physical materials containing written words, photographs, illustrations, and designed layouts.

  • Newspapers and magazines
  • Books, textbooks, and journals
  • Brochures, leaflets, and posters
StrengthsCan be read without an internet connection; easy to keep, annotate, and revisit; often provides space for detailed explanations.
WeaknessesUpdates cannot be made instantly; printing and distribution cost resources; outdated copies may continue circulating.

Best used when: readers need a lasting reference, detailed explanation, or focused reading experience.

Gallery 02 · Audio and video transmission◖

Broadcast Media

Broadcast media sends audio or audiovisual programs to large audiences through scheduled or live transmission.

  • Radio news and public service announcements
  • Television news and educational programs
  • Live interviews and public broadcasts
StrengthsCan reach many people at once; sound and visuals make events easier to follow; live reports can provide timely information.
WeaknessesAudiences may have limited control over the schedule; airtime is limited; editing and presentation choices can influence how a story is understood.

Best used when: information needs to reach a broad audience through spoken explanations, visuals, or live coverage.

Gallery 03 · Connected information⌘

Digital Media

Digital media is information created, stored, accessed, or distributed using electronic devices and digital systems.

  • Websites and online articles
  • E-books and digital learning modules
  • Email, online databases, and presentations
StrengthsInformation can be searched and updated quickly; files are easy to store and share; links can connect readers to additional sources.
WeaknessesAccess may depend on devices, electricity, and connectivity; online content can contain errors; privacy and security need attention.

Best used when: users need searchable resources, convenient storage, collaboration, or frequently updated material.

Gallery 04 · Participatory platforms◎

New Media

New media uses networked digital platforms where audiences can interact, respond, publish, and take part in conversations.

  • Social networking platforms
  • Blogs, podcasts, and discussion forums
  • Livestreams and user-created content
StrengthsAllows two-way communication; gives individuals and communities a place to share perspectives; can build networks and support collaboration.
WeaknessesUnverified claims can spread widely; comment sections may become disrespectful; algorithms and popularity can affect what users see.

Best used when: participation, community discussion, feedback, and sharing different perspectives are important.

Gallery 05 · Moving-image storytelling▶

Film and Video Media

Film and video combine moving images, sound, narration, and editing to tell stories or explain subjects.

  • Documentaries and short films
  • Educational and instructional videos
  • News features and recorded interviews
StrengthsCan demonstrate processes visually; combines sound and images to hold attention; can communicate emotion and complex ideas clearly.
WeaknessesProduction can require time and equipment; editing may leave out important context; viewers may need a suitable device and enough data or connectivity.

Best used when: a process, event, personal account, or visual example is easier to understand by watching.

Gallery 06 · Public-space communication▥

Outdoor Media

Outdoor media displays messages in shared physical spaces where people encounter them during everyday activities.

  • Billboards and transit advertisements
  • Community notices and public posters
  • Wayfinding signs and information displays
StrengthsVisible to people in public areas; can communicate a short message quickly; does not require an individual device or internet connection.
WeaknessesSpace for detail is limited; viewers may only see it briefly; placement, weather, and visibility can affect how well it works.

Best used when: a short reminder, public announcement, direction, or awareness message needs broad local visibility.

03 / SIDE-BY-SIDE

Choosing the right medium

The most suitable medium depends on the audience, purpose, urgency, available resources, and level of detail required.

Media formUseful forWhat to watch out for
PrintLong explanations and permanent referencesPublication date and whether newer information exists
BroadcastWide-reach announcements and live reportingSource credibility, editing, and missing context
DigitalResearch, storage, and fast updatesWebsite reliability, privacy, and inaccurate content
New mediaDiscussion, participation, and community updatesUnverified posts, harmful comments, and viral rumors
Film / VideoDemonstrations, visual storytelling, and tutorialsEditing choices, missing context, and production quality
OutdoorLocal reminders, directions, and public awarenessReadability, visibility, and limited explanation space

A medium is not automatically reliable just because it is familiar or professionally presented. Always evaluate the actual message and its source.

04 / REAL-LIFE APPLICATION

One community announcement, six ways

Imagine a school is organizing a community clean-up drive. How could different media forms help inform students, parents, and residents?

Exhibit scenario

A neighborhood clean-up is happening this Saturday.

The announcement needs to explain the date, meeting place, purpose, and what participants should expect. Each medium can contribute in a different way.

Print

Flyers can be posted on school bulletin boards and distributed to nearby households with the event details.

Broadcast

A school radio segment or local announcement can remind a large audience about the schedule.

Digital

The school website can provide the full program, contact details, and updates if arrangements change.

New media

An official school social account can invite questions, share reminders, and encourage community participation.

Film / Video

A short video can show the purpose of the activity and introduce the planned community project.

Outdoor

A clear poster near the community center can remind passersby of the event location and time.

Key insight: Repeating consistent information across suitable channels can help reach different audiences. The organizer should keep the date, venue, and instructions accurate in every version.

05 / CRITICAL THINKING

Use the CLEAR check before you believe or share

A practical five-step guide for examining information. Do not judge a post by its appearance or number of reactions alone.

C

Check the creator

Who made the message? Is the author or organization identifiable and qualified to discuss the topic?

L

Look for evidence

Are there facts, documents, links, or credible sources supporting the claims?

E

Examine the date

Is the information current? Could an old post or image be presented as a new event?

A

Assess the purpose

Is the message meant to inform, persuade, entertain, sell, or provoke a reaction?

R

Review other sources

Can independent, trustworthy sources confirm the same important details?

If important details cannot be confirmed, pause before sharing. For urgent public announcements, check the relevant official organization or local government source.

08 / FINAL THOUGHT

Leave the museum as a mindful media user.

Media gives people opportunities to learn, connect, and participate. Its value depends not only on the technology, but also on the choices of the people who create and consume it.

Think beyond the headline.

Ask questions. Check evidence. Respect other people. Protect personal information. Give credit to original creators. Before passing information along, consider whether it is accurate, useful, and fair to the people who may be affected.
